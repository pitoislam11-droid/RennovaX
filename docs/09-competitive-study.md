# 09 — Competitive study

Five products worth understanding properly. The instruction is to learn, not to copy. For
each: what it proves, what it does not solve, and what Rennova takes from it.

Facts below were checked in September 2026; specifics such as pricing change, so treat
numbers as indicative rather than current.

---

## 1. MyBuilder

**What it is.** A UK marketplace where homeowners post a job through a simple form and
interested tradespeople get in touch. Free for homeowners; tradespeople pay per lead, on a
model where a fee is triggered when the homeowner shows genuine intent by shortlisting,
rather than on every introduction.

**What it proves.** The marketplace shape works. Homeowners will post jobs and wait for
trades to come to them, and trades will pay for access to intent. Charging on shortlist
rather than on contact is a thoughtful alignment — it means the contractor pays for interest,
not for a click.

**What it does not solve.** The job itself is still whatever the homeowner managed to type
into one form. The entire information-gathering burden remains with the contractor, so the
call/photos/visit cycle is untouched. Quality of posts is the platform's structural weak
point.

**What Rennova takes.** The marketplace model and the principle of charging on intent rather
than on access. **Where Rennova goes further:** we do not ask the homeowner to describe the
job. We take over the job of producing the description.

---

## 2. Checkatrade

**What it is.** Primarily a searchable, reviewed directory. Homeowners enter a postcode and
trade, browse profiles and reviews, and make contact. Trades pay a membership to be listed
and pass background checks to join; tiered plans, with lead-generating tiers above the basic
listing.

**What it proves.** **Trust and verification carry enormous weight** in this category, and
homeowners will pay attention to them. Checkatrade's brand equity is almost entirely a trust
asset. The vetting-plus-reviews combination is the thing homeowners actually want, because
their dominant fear is being ripped off.

**What it does not solve.** The core object is a company, not a project. Once the homeowner
has a phone number, the platform's job ends and the entire information problem starts. It
also produces the cold-call experience: a homeowner contacting several trades receives
several conversations, each starting from zero.

**What Rennova takes.** The seriousness about verification, and the understanding that
specific, checkable claims beat generic badges. **Where Rennova goes further:** we combine
that trust layer with a structured project and a sealed quoting process, so trust is
established *and* the homeowner does not have to run the process themselves.

---

## 3. Houzz and Houzz Pro

**What it is.** Two products joined. Consumer-side Houzz is inspiration and discovery at
enormous scale — photography of finished projects, saved to idea books, leading to
professionals. Houzz Pro is contractor software: estimating, and a takeoff tool where pros
mark up plans to measure areas, lengths, counts and volumes, with AI-assisted plan reading
that generates measurements automatically and feeds them into estimate line items.

**What it proves.** Two separate things, both important.

First, **beautiful project photography is the engine of trust and discovery** in home
improvement. People decide emotionally from images long before they evaluate a company.

Second, **the takeoff-to-estimate pipeline is the contractor's real workflow.** Measurements
are not an end in themselves; they are the input to a priced line item. Houzz Pro's value is
compressing measurement → quantity → price.

**What it does not solve.** The two halves are only loosely connected. The homeowner-facing
discovery experience does not produce the structured measurements the contractor-facing
estimating tool consumes. The contractor still originates the measurement data themselves,
typically from plans or a site visit.

**What Rennova takes.** Portfolio photography as a first-class product surface, and the
recognition that measurements must land in a form that shortens estimating rather than just
looking impressive. **Where Rennova goes further:** we connect the two halves. Data captured
by the homeowner flows directly into the contractor's pricing context.

---

## 4. Hover — *very important reference*

**What it is.** The homeowner or a field rep walks around a house taking a guided set of
smartphone photos — typically eight to sixteen, one of each side and corner, with in-app
guidance on angle, distance and safety. Hover returns a dimensioned 3D model of the exterior
with measurements for roofing, siding, windows and openings, usually within about an hour,
and feeds those into estimating and visualisation.

**What it proves.** This is the closest existing proof of Rennova's central bet:

> **An ordinary person, given precise instructions about which photos to take, will produce
> a dataset good enough for professional measurement and pricing.**

Three details are worth internalising. The instruction set is small — the magic is in
*which* photos, not how many. Guidance is delivered in the moment, over the camera, not as a
list to memorise. And processing is asynchronous, which removes any need for on-device
real-time reconstruction.

**What it does not solve.** Hover is exterior-focused and measurement-focused. It does not
address intent, scope, condition, preference or the marketplace around the work. It answers
"how big is this house" rather than "what does this person want done and what will it cost".

**What Rennova takes.** The guided-capture principle, wholesale, and the asynchronous
processing model — capture fast on the phone, do heavy work on a server, deliver the result
shortly after. **Where Rennova goes further:** we apply the principle to interiors and to
*scope* rather than only geometry, and we carry it through to a quotable job pack.

---

## 5. Magicplan — *very important reference*

**What it is.** A contractor-facing app that uses AR and, on LiDAR devices, automatic
detection to build scaled floor plans. Point at corners and walls and it measures in real
time; on LiDAR devices it detects walls, doors, windows and outlets automatically. Sketches
are described as roughly 95% accurate, with Bluetooth laser meter pairing available for
near-exact measurement. Widely used by remodellers, restoration contractors and insurance
adjusters, and it exports to CAD and into estimating workflows.

**What it proves.** Room scanning on a phone is a solved, shipped, commercially viable
capability, and non-specialists can operate it. Also, crucially: **laser integration exists
precisely because AR alone is not accurate enough for every purpose.** A mature product in
this space still offers a precision fallback, which validates our measurement ladder.

**What it does not solve.** Magicplan is a tool for professionals. It assumes the operator
knows what they are surveying and why. Hand it to a homeowner with no trade knowledge and
they will not know what matters. It produces a plan, not a scope of works, and there is no
marketplace attached.

**What Rennova takes.** The technical approach to room capture, the honesty of a laser
fallback, and evidence that the UX can be made simple. **Where Rennova goes further:** our
operator is the homeowner, not the professional, so guidance must carry the domain knowledge
the operator lacks, and the output is a priceable project rather than a drawing.

## The synthesis

Each reference solves one piece:

| | Solves |
|---|---|
| MyBuilder | Marketplace mechanics and intent-based pricing |
| Checkatrade | Trust, verification, reviews |
| Houzz / Houzz Pro | Portfolio discovery, and measurement feeding estimates |
| Hover | Guided capture by an untrained person produces professional data |
| Magicplan | Phone-based room scanning with honest precision fallbacks |

Nobody joins them up. The gap Rennova occupies is:

> **Guided capture by an untrained homeowner (Hover's principle) using room-scanning
> technology (Magicplan's capability), producing a structured scope of works, delivered into
> a trusted marketplace (Checkatrade's asset) with sealed quoting and a portfolio-driven
> storefront (Houzz's strength), replacing the unstructured job post (MyBuilder's weak
> point).**

The defensible position is not any single capability. It is the **structured, provenance-
tagged corpus of real projects** that accumulates in the middle, and the capture plans that
get measurably better every time a contractor tells us what was missing.
